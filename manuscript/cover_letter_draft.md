# Cover letter draft

Dear Editors,

This cover letter is addressed to the handling editor for exclusive submission to Communications Earth & Environment. The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere and is not currently under review by another journal.

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, **"A process-terrain screening framework for coastal flood susceptibility using open water-level, meteorological and terrain data"**, for consideration.

Coastal flood screening is often driven by extreme water-level indicators, yet flood susceptibility also depends on whether the relevant water-level processes align with connected lowland terrain. In this manuscript, we treat this as a process-terrain coupling problem and combine four open data streams: the Global Storm Surge Reconstruction (GSSR), COAST-RP storm-tide return levels, Open-Meteo ERA5-derived meteorological drivers, and DeltaDTM coastal terrain.

The study makes three main contributions. First, it keeps surge residuals and storm-tide return levels physically distinct, showing that their 10-year indicators diverge strongly at several European tide-dominated sites while remaining much closer at selected comparison sites. Second, it evaluates meteorological coherence of reconstructed daily surge residuals using station-centred pressure, wind and precipitation diagnostics. Third, it introduces an ocean-connected DeltaDTM terrain-sensitivity screen that excludes isolated low cells and distinguishes low terrain from marine access.

The resulting process-terrain typology shows that large water-level indicators do not automatically identify the strongest terrain sensitivity. In the present site set, Sheerness emerges as the clearest aligned hotspot, while Hoek van Holland, Newlyn, Brest and Aberdeen illustrate terrain-buffered or decoupled cases once ocean connectivity is enforced. This framing is intended as a reproducible screening approach rather than a local flood-risk or hydrodynamic forecast.

All data streams used in the analysis are public, and the manuscript is accompanied locally by figure source tables, reproducibility scripts, a publication-readiness audit, and a compact package-generation workflow. The analysis explicitly avoids treating GSSR and COAST-RP as mutual validation targets and avoids interpreting static terrain sensitivity as observed or predicted flood extent.

We believe the manuscript will be of interest to readers concerned with coastal flood screening, open environmental data integration, terrain uncertainty, and reproducible hazard assessment. It provides a transparent example of how physically distinct open datasets can be combined without collapsing them into a single overinterpreted hazard metric.
